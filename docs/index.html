    The big idea

    Leverage the cross-modal correlations in wearable sensor data.

    Analysis of wearable data streams faces two fundamental challenges: the lack of gold-standard labels and incomplete sensor data.

    01Missing gold-standard labels+

    Unlike computer vision and natural language processing, wearable health data analysis often relies on small clinical studies with missing gold-standard labels. Self-supervised learning addresses this by learning meaningful representations from unlabeled data.

    02Synchronized masking limits cross-modal learning+

    Synchronized masking removes the same time windows across all modalities simultaneously. With all modality information at a time point removed at once, the model relies more on temporal correlations than on inter-modality relationships.

    03Cross-modality masking+

    MoCA masks patches from different modalities independently across time windows. The model infers missing signals in one modality from the remaining modalities at the same or nearby time points.

    04A theoretical link to kernel CCA+

    MAE reconstruction reduces to maximizing the alignment between the unmasked and masked views, a kernel canonical correlation analysis (KCCA) problem. Synchronized masking weakens this association because no modality remains available at a given time point. MoCA preserves cross-modal cues, so the learned representations better capture patterns that span multiple sensing modalities.

    “Cross-modality masking encourages the encoder to learn richer representations that preserve both within-modality structure and cross-modality relationships.”

    What we found

    Stronger representations, in and out of distribution.

    With the encoder frozen, MoCA reaches 93.1% linear probing accuracy on UCI-HAR, its pre-training dataset, ahead of HAR-Net, pre-trained on 700,000 person-days of UK Biobank data, and MAE Synchronized Masking.

    On six out-of-distribution datasets, MoCA achieves the top accuracy on five, transferring from six-channel pre-training (accelerometer + gyroscope) to accelerometer-only data. Its masking strategy and pre-training design yield stronger representations immediately useful for downstream tasks.

    How it works

    Two stages: learn an encoder, then classify with it

    Stage 1 pre-trains the encoder on unlabeled data by masked reconstruction. Stage 2 uses the encoder’s representations to train an activity classifier.

      Stage 1 · Pre-training

      Masked autoencoding on unlabeled data. Patches are hidden independently across sensors and the decoder reconstructs them. The reconstruction error trains the encoder; afterwards the decoder is discarded.

    2. 2

      Stage 2 · Classification

      Labeled windows pass through the pre-trained encoder, and a classification head maps its representation to the activity. Linear probing trains only the head; fine-tuning also updates the encoder.
